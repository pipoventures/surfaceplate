# Decision Record

- Decision ID: DR-87
- Date: 2026-09-11
- Application: Surfaceplate (the framework itself) — the relationship between the two adoption
  routes, and what the file-based one is allowed to be unable to express.
- Decision owner: Mario Pipo (maintainer), who asked for the AI-assisted walkthrough to be reviewed
  for *"areas or parameters that have been successful just because we used AI but had the human done
  it they would have likely failed"*, and on the result: *"Let's fix all of them."* Drafted by the
  agent under `ACT-106`; confirmed by merging.
- Status: accepted — implemented by `ACT-106`.
- Risk level: 3 — it adds a command-line flag to a published interface and changes when the wizard
  validates a gate artefact. No control's assertion changes.
- Related work item: `ACT-106`; closes `F165`, `F166`, `F167`, `F168`, `F169`.

## Decision

**`--propose`/`--answers` is a second implementation of the interview, and every answer the
interactive route can take, it must be able to take too.** Where it cannot, that is a defect in the
file-based route, not a limitation of it.

Three consequences, all implemented:

1. **A decision the interview asks for must be answerable on the non-interactive route.**
   `stack.builds_user_interface` was not, so `--builds-ui` exists.
2. **A refusal must name the adopter's own line.** An answer of the wrong shape is refused where the
   record is read, not three layers down where only an internal profile path remains.
3. **The two routes must agree about what exists.** A gate artefact that a scaffold offer will
   create counts as present in both, whichever field names it.

## Context

An AI-assisted adoption was run end to end against a real repository, and the transcript reviewed
against the code. Five defects came out of it. What they have in common is the argument for this
record: **all five were survived by reading the tool's source, and none of them by reading its
documentation.**

The route was built for people who find the terminal hard (`DR-85`). On the evidence of this
walkthrough it was, for two of the five, the only route that worked at all — which inverts its
purpose and, worse, hides the defect: an agent reads `scaffold.py`, finds the seeds, gets through,
and never reports that the documented path was a dead end.

### Why `builds_user_interface` is the sharpest of the five

The answers record told the reader, in its own header: *"answer stack.builds_user_interface and run
`--propose --level` again to see the interface gates."* `propose()` reads the repository, not the
record. The answer was discarded on the next run, silently.

There was no other route. The field is decisive rather than descriptive — `true` makes all four
interface gates `required` — and three of those four have no seed, so they cannot be scaffolded
either. **A repository that builds a user interface could not be adopted through this route at
all**, and the instruction printed in the file was the thing that could not work.

### Why `effective_from` is the most serious

`F47` — *"A freshly adopted repository violates its own gate on the first check"* — reads `Closed`
in this register. Its remedy, that a gate binds from the instant of adoption rather than from that
midnight, was applied where a scaffold creates the artefact and **not** where the adopter names one.
So the symptom came back for the adopter who supplies their own artefacts, and not for the one who
accepts every offer. The diligent path was the penalised one.

It is worse than a returning symptom, because the obvious correction is foreclosed. Normalising the
dates after the profile is committed reads to `SP034` as gate-widening — moving an effective date
forward to make a past violation disappear — and `SP034` is never graced. The walkthrough tried
exactly that, turned a graced `WARN` into an ungraced `FAIL`, and escaped only because it had not
committed first. **An adopter who commits first has no way back except seven exception records.**

`SP034` is right to be unforgiving here; a good-faith correction and a bad-faith one produce the
same diff, which is the point of the rule. That is precisely why the proposal must be right the
first time.

## Impact

- `surfaceplate adopt --builds-ui {yes,no}`, honoured by `--propose` and recorded as the human's own
  answer. The record's header names the flag and states plainly that editing the line and
  re-proposing will not work.
- `defaults.propose_gates` proposes `flow.adoption_moment`, not `flow.adoption_date`.
- `wizard.replay` refuses a list where one value belongs, naming the line.
- `flow._first_problem` exempts any path an accepted offer will create, not only the field whose own
  origin is `scaffolded`.
- `core/PREREQUISITE_GATES.md` gains a section naming `.standards/seeds/` — installed, readable, and
  until now mentioned in no document an adopter receives.

## Evidence

- Every fix has a regression test that fails when the fix is reverted and names the finding.
- `F168`'s negative control reproduces the walkthrough's exact refusal: *"the review refuses to
  write: gates.register_currency.artefact: Nothing exists at that path in this repository."*
- `F166` is additionally enforced by the combinatorial matrix across its 208 runs, whose own
  expectation encoded the defect (`adopt_matrix.py` expected `flow.adoption_date`).
- `--builds-ui yes` verified by effect on a real repository: the four interface gates appear in the
  record, and `stack.builds_user_interface` is recorded as answered rather than `needs-human`.

## Limitations

- **This does not make the two routes equivalent; it makes them equivalent on five known points.**
  Nothing checks the general property. A field added to the interview in future can still be
  unreachable from the record, and the check that would catch it does not exist.
- `--builds-ui` is a flag rather than the record being read back. Reading the record back is what
  its header used to imply and would be the more general answer; it is not taken here because
  `--propose` regenerating from discovery is what makes it honest about the repository, and
  re-reading raises a question this record does not answer — which of the adopter's edits survive a
  re-proposal, and which are silently replaced by a new discovery.
- **`F166` narrows the audit window for every future adopter**, from the adoption day's midnight to
  the adoption instant. That is `ACT-035`'s decision applied consistently rather than a new one, and
  it is a reduction in history audited. It is recorded here rather than left implicit.
- The walkthrough's own conduct is not evidence that the agent contract holds in general. It held in
  this run, with this model, on this prompt.
