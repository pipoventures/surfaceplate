# Decision Record

- Decision ID: DR-79
- Date: 2026-09-09
- Application: Surfaceplate (the framework itself) — which version the adoption-readiness
  programme publishes as, and what `1.0` is reserved for.
- Decision owner: Mario Pipo (maintainer), stated in this session: *"it will be 0.18.0. v1.0 is
  reserved for after the independent review."*
- Status: accepted — implemented by `ACT-084`.
- Risk level: 1 — a version number and the documents that state it. No behaviour changes.
- Related work item: `ACT-084`; **completes `DR-61`'s reasoning** about the alpha classifier;
  bears on `H6` and `RELEASE_PLAN` item 10.

## Decision

**The adoption-readiness programme publishes as `0.18.0`. `1.0` is reserved until the independent
review (`H6`) has happened.**

## Context and alternatives

The programme delivered five surfaces of `DR-69`, closed twenty-one findings, and added an
uninstall path, a currency check and a pathway sweep with its own report. It would be a defensible
`1.0` on volume. It is not one on **assurance**, and the difference is the whole point of this
framework.

**Rejected: publishing this as `1.0`.** `DR-61` already binds the `Development Status :: 3 - Alpha`
classifier to item 10 — the independent audit — and states that *"the classifier moves to
Production/Stable only with the release that item 10 authorises."* Publishing `1.0` while the
package's own metadata declares itself alpha would put **two answers in one artefact**, which is
precisely the defect (`F117`, `F118`, `F127`) this programme spent its length removing from
everything else. A framework whose selling point is that it refuses to say more than it can show
cannot open its own 1.0 announcement with a contradiction.

`org/decisions/README.md` records that **no independent validator exists for this repository**.
`1.0` from a project that has never been reviewed by anyone but its author is a claim about
maturity that nothing behind it supports.

**Rejected: waiting for the review before publishing anything.** That is the deadlock `H16` is
already in. The review packet must be regenerated from a **published commit** — the generator
refuses an archive whose inner manifest is not the published anchor — and the packet must describe
the post-restructure corpus, because the maintainer's own 5 September ruling forbids sending a
reviewer the pre-restructure one. So publishing is a **precondition** of the review, not a reward
for it. `0.18.0` unblocks `H16` → `H4` → `H6` without claiming what `H6` has not yet granted.

## Impact

- `surfaceplate/VERSION`, `pyproject.toml` and `README.md`'s version line move to `0.18.0`; the
  changelog section is retitled. The `Development Status :: 3 - Alpha` classifier **stays**, per
  `DR-61`.
- Historical prose naming `0.17.0` is left alone. `check_conformance.py`'s *"a record written
  before 0.17.0 reads correctly"* and the version-comparison examples in the suites are statements
  about a boundary and about ordering; changing them would make them false.
- The sequence this unblocks: publish `0.18.0` → regenerate the review packet from that commit →
  `H16` sends it → `H4` (Part A, the digest recomputation) and `H6` (Part B, the audit) → and only
  then is `1.0` a claim with something behind it.

## Evidence

- `DR-61`: *"The classifier moves to Production/Stable only with the release that item 10
  authorises."* Verified still present in `pyproject.toml`: `classifiers = ["Development Status ::
  3 - Alpha"]`.
- `H4`'s prepared notes record that the packet generator *"refuses any archive whose inner manifest
  is not the published one"*, which is why publication precedes review rather than following it.
- `check_code_registers.py` asserts `README.md`'s stated version equals `surfaceplate/VERSION`, so
  the three places cannot drift apart unnoticed.

## Limitations and follow-up

**A version number is not a maturity claim anyone is obliged to read that way**, and `0.18.0` will
be adopted by whoever adopts it regardless of what it is called. The reservation is honest
signalling, not a control; nothing prevents someone treating `0.18.0` as production-ready, and
nothing should.

**`1.0` is not automatic once `H6` returns.** The audit may produce findings, and those findings
are the input to that decision rather than a formality on the way to it.

## Approval

Stated by the maintainer directly. No agent chose this, and no release is authorised by this
record — publishing `0.18.0` remains a separate act that only the maintainer can take.
