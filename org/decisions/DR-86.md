# Decision Record

- Decision ID: DR-86
- Date: 2026-09-11
- Application: Surfaceplate (the framework itself) — how `org/FINDINGS.md` records a limitation
  that has been decided about and has not gone away.
- Decision owner: Mario Pipo (maintainer), who put the question: *"close F55 if it can't be
  fixed"*, and on `F131`, *"why open… if it is something I need to check periodically [that] is
  an action that I should have in my calendar but not a finding."* Drafted by the agent under
  `ACT-102`; confirmed by merging.
- Status: accepted — implemented by `ACT-102`.
- Risk level: 2 — it changes a convention of this repository's own register, and nothing else.
- Related work item: `ACT-102`; moves `F55` and `F131` to `Accepted`; raises `H26`.

## Decision

**`org/FINDINGS.md` has three statuses: `Open`, `Accepted`, `Closed`.**

| Status | Means |
|---|---|
| `Open` | Outstanding. Work is pending and someone can do it |
| `Accepted` | Decided, no action pending, **and the condition persists** |
| `Closed` | Fixed. The condition is gone |

**An `Accepted` finding must state what would reopen it.** `tests/check_code_registers.py` refuses
one that does not.

## Context

The maintainer was right twice, in opposite directions, and the two together are the argument.

On `F55` — narrative docstrings can drift, remedied by a habit rather than a test — he asked why it
was open when it could not be fixed. **`Open` implies work pending, and there was none.** A reader
scanning the register for what to do next was being misled by two of its three open rows.

On `F131` — a CVE in a pinned test dependency, accepted at `H21` — he observed that a periodic
upstream check *"is an action that I should have in my calendar but not a finding"*. Right about
the check, which is now `H26`. But the limitation is not the check: this package **still ships a
pinned dependency carrying an unpatched CVE**, and `CONTROL_PRINCIPLES.md:7` requires limitations
to be recorded rather than smoothed away. An independent reviewer at `H6` must meet that as a
recorded fact, not infer it from a version pin.

So neither existing word worked. `Closed` in the index column would read exactly like a defect that
was repaired — and the register's one job is answering *"what is still wrong here?"*

**`F131`'s own body had already argued this**, and was left standing as the reason to keep it open:
*"an accepted risk and a fixed defect are different states, and a register that recorded them the
same way would be unable to answer 'what is still wrong here?'"* That reasoning was sound and the
conclusion drawn from it was wrong, because only two states were on offer. The third resolves it.

### Why an `Accepted` finding must say what would reopen it

Otherwise acceptance is abandonment with better manners. `F131`'s is an upstream release;
`F55`'s is a third docstring found wrong about its own code — which would be evidence the habit is
not working and something mechanical is needed after all. Both are falsifiable and neither is a
date, which is the point: a review trigger tied to a calendar expires quietly, a trigger tied to a
condition does not.

### Scope: this register, not the standard

**No adopter inherits these three words.** `assurance_findings` is a pattern-A control — the
checker requires the register to exist, be tracked, be non-empty and carry no placeholder, and
never reads its contents. The agent's own option text when this was put to the maintainer said
*"adopters inherit it"*, and that was wrong; the correction was made before implementation.

Whether the standard should prescribe a status vocabulary is a real question and is deliberately
**not** answered here. It would put words in every adopter's register on the strength of one
repository's experience of needing them, which is the kind of generalisation `DR-80` argues
against.

## Impact

- `org/FINDINGS.md` gains a status-convention section; `F55` and `F131` move to `Accepted`.
- `tests/check_code_registers.py`: `word()` recognises three statuses rather than two, and a new
  check refuses an `Accepted` finding that does not say what would reopen it.
- `org/HUMAN_ACTIONS.md`: `H26`, the quarterly upstream check `F131` used to justify staying open.

## Evidence

- Both directions: the register passes with the new statuses, and removing `F131`'s "what would
  reopen it" line makes the suite fail and name the finding.
- The body/index agreement check (`F72`) is unchanged and still compares the two, now over three
  words instead of two.

## Limitations

- **`Accepted` is a claim about intent, and nothing verifies intent.** A finding parked there to
  avoid fixing it looks identical to one genuinely accepted. The reopen condition is the only
  guard, and it is prose — a check can require that the words are present, never that they are the
  right words.
- The status lives in two places per finding (index row, body line) and is kept in step by a check
  rather than by structure. That predates this decision and is unchanged by it.
