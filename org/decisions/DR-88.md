# Decision Record

- Decision ID: DR-88
- Date: 2026-09-11
- Application: Surfaceplate (the framework itself) — whether PyPI's PEP 740 attestations become a
  stated part of this framework's integrity story.
- Decision owner: Mario Pipo (maintainer), answering `H28` in his own words: *"I don't want these
  attestations."* Drafted by the agent under `ACT-112`; confirmed by merging.
- Status: accepted — implemented by `ACT-112`.
- Risk level: 2 — it changes what this framework says about its own assurance, and changes no
  mechanism. Nothing is added, removed, or checked differently.
- Related work item: `ACT-112`; answers `H28`; closes `F173`. Revisits `DR-14`'s rejection on a
  changed premise without amending `DR-14`.

## Decision

**PyPI's attestations are not adopted as part of what this framework claims about its own
integrity.** They are not surfaced by `doctor`, not described in the review packet, and not offered
as evidence toward `F6`.

**They are recorded as existing, here, and that is the whole of the change.** Since `0.16.0`, every
published artefact carries a Sigstore-signed `publish/v1` attestation naming the repository, the
workflow and the environment that built it, with the artefact digest as its subject. Anyone may
verify one. This project simply does not build an argument on it.

## Context

`H28` was raised because `DR-14` had considered attestations and rejected them:

> *"Rejected: sign the manifest, or adopt PEP 740 attestations… Rejected here because **key custody
> and a signing process are infrastructure**, which `DR-12`'s permanent boundary forecloses."*

Trusted publishing supplies them with neither. **The cost that rejection turned on is zero, and has
been since the first publish** — so the decision needed retaking on its merits rather than standing
on a premise that had quietly expired. That is `F173`.

### Why the answer is still no

**An attestation establishes authenticity, not honesty — and this framework is about honesty.**
`DR-14` said so before the question was live, and it remains the whole of the argument. A party with
write access commits a payload and a manifest that agree, the workflow faithfully builds them, and
PyPI faithfully attests that it did. Every signature in that chain is valid and the contents are
still whatever that party chose. `F6` is about the second thing and no attestation reaches it.

**Claiming it would buy credibility the mechanism has not earned.** A reader who sees "signed,
attested, verified against a transparency log" beside a governance framework will reasonably infer
more assurance than exists. This project's entire subject is the gap between a control that holds and
one that merely reports that it does. Advertising a cryptographic guarantee about the wrong property
would be that failure, committed by the tool that names it.

**Nothing is lost by declining.** The attestations exist and are public. A reviewer who wants them
can find them in one request, and this record tells them the project knows and chose not to lean on
it — which is a better answer to *"why doesn't it mention these?"* than silence.

### What was rejected

**Rejected: surface attestations in `doctor`.** It reads as a feature and would be one. It would
also put a green line in front of an adopter that speaks to provenance while sitting among lines
that speak to conformance, and the adopter would not be expected to know the difference.

**Rejected: regenerate and re-send the review packet.** The `0.18.0` packet omits attestations and
has been sent to two reviewers; its digest is quoted in both emails and in the published release
notes. Silently replacing a document someone was asked to hash is worse than the omission it would
fix. This record is what a reviewer who finds the attestations should be pointed at instead.

## Impact

- `org/FINDINGS.md`: `F173` closes. `F6`'s qualification from `ACT-110` stands unchanged — the title
  is imprecise and stays, because it is quoted in the published release notes and in invitations
  already sent.
- No code changes. No payload changes. No control changes.
- `org/HUMAN_ACTIONS.md`: `H28` moves to decided.

## Evidence

The facts this decision rests on were established rather than recalled, and are quoted in `F173`:
the attestation fetched from PyPI's integrity endpoint and parsed for `0.16.0`, `0.16.1` and
`0.18.0`; the subject digest compared against a sdist digest derived independently from a fresh
download; the packet's omission established by `grep` across the generator, the generated page and
`audit/AUDIT_SCOPE.md`.

## Limitations

- **This decision is about what the framework CLAIMS, and claims are not mechanisms.** Nothing here
  makes the attestations weaker or stronger. A reviewer may reasonably weigh them anyway, and should.
- **It does not close `F6` and does not try to.** The finding needs a party other than this
  repository to establish a value about the contents, not about the build.
- **The reasoning would change if PEP 740 grew a content-level predicate** — an attestation that
  said something about what the artefact contains rather than where it came from. This record
  declines what exists today, not the idea.
