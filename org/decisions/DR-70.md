## Decision

**`DR-2`'s decision stands. Its scope is narrowed to this repository.**

`DR-2` declared a private doctrine bundle *"sole behavioural canon for AI-assisted work **under
this standard**"*. Read literally, that binds every adopter of a public standard to a bundle they
cannot read, have not agreed to, and in most cases do not know exists. That is wider than a
published standard can carry.

The amended scope, and the whole of the change:

1. **The bundle is canon for this repository's own AI-assisted work, and for nothing else.** Not
   for the standard, not for its adopters, and not citable to an adopter as part of it.
2. **`DR-2`'s substance is not re-argued and does not change.** Its reasoning — that a flat merge
   collapses a tier architecture, so a reader cannot tell an invariant from an overridable default
   — was correct when written and remains correct. The defect was one of scope, not of judgement.
3. **This repository is exercising the same right any adopter has**, not a privilege the framework
   grants its author. Topic 1 states the general rule: an adopter may declare what governs their
   own repository, binding that repository and no other. `DR-2`, so scoped, is this repository's
   instance of it.

## Context and alternatives

**`DR-4` already required this position; `DR-2` was the document out of step with it.** `DR-4`
records, as a consequence of its own decision and not as a separate finding, that what the product
ships *"is necessarily a reference kernel — a generic, unopinionated starting point — not any
individual maintainer's personal, machine-specific doctrine. A personal kernel, with an
individual's own tier choices and overrides, becomes an example of an adopter profile."* That is
this record's conclusion, written eight days earlier, in a record that was never reconciled with
`DR-2`. So this is not a new position being adopted; it is two accepted records being made to
agree, and `DR-2` is the one that moves because `DR-4` is the one that was right.

**Rejected: rewriting `DR-2`'s 2026-08-30 text.** A decision record is a log of what was decided
and when. Editing the decision itself would destroy the record of a real scope error that stood
for nine days, which is exactly the retrospective edit this repository's own rules forbid. `DR-2`'s
*status line* is corrected in place — a status is a current fact — and its decision text is left
exactly as written. Topic 1's supersession rule, shipped in `ACT-065`, states that split; this
record is its first application to a decision record.

**Rejected: raising a finding number for this.** `F125` already records the adopter-facing half —
that the standard ships no precedence rule at all — and the over-wide scope is tracked as `WI-1` in
the programme this session is executing. A third identifier for one defect, being corrected in the
same change that names it, is bookkeeping rather than a record. Stated here so the absence is a
decision rather than an oversight.

## Impact

- Numerical/model output: none. Contracts/schemas: none — the *mechanism* by which a repository
  declares its canon is `WI-2`, a separate, additive schema change; this record is the scope
  correction only.
- Security/data: none.
- Adopter-facing: this is the point of it. No adopter is, or ever was in practice, bound by the

  record is what an adopter reads.
- Documentation: `DR-2`'s status line; this record.

## Evidence

`DR-2`'s own text, read directly at `d66a8ec`: *"is sole behavioural canon for AI-assisted work
under this standard"* (`DR-2.md:18-19`). `DR-4`'s consequence paragraph, quoted above, read at the
same commit. No code, schema, or check changes, so there is nothing further to verify by effect —
and stating that plainly is better than manufacturing a test for a documentary correction.

## Limitations and follow-up

**`DR-2`'s stated follow-up is now impossible as written, and this record closes it rather than
leaving it owing.** `DR-2` required that `core/AI_OPERATING_MODEL.md` be rewritten into "an
explicit, dated projection" of the bundle. That document no longer exists: `ACT-068` retired it
under `DR-69`, and its content now lives in Topics 2, 3, 5 and 6, authored as this standard's own
rules in their own right rather than as a projection of anything. The obligation is discharged by
the restructure, not by the rewrite `DR-2` anticipated — **and the rewrite was never done in the
nine days it stood owing**, which is the more useful fact about it. A follow-up that outlives the
document it names is one nobody was going to do.

What remains genuinely open is the adopter-facing mechanism — how a repository *declares* its
canon in a way the checker can read — which is `WI-2` and is not this record's to settle.
