# Decision Record

- Decision ID: DR-35
- Date: 2026-09-01
- Application: Surfaceplate (the framework itself) — `surfaceplate/adopt/`, the CLI wizard
  `RELEASE_PLAN.md` item 2 shipped.
- Decision owner: Mario Pipo (maintainer), per the decision-maker convention recorded in
  `org/decisions/README.md`.
- Status: accepted — Phase 1 shipped in this same change (`ACT-026`); Phase 2 scoped, not started.
- Risk level: 3 — it revises a design decision (`DR-32`) after real evidence contradicted the
  reasoning behind it, and it changes what every future adopter is asked, and how, across all 19
  gates and every control.
- Related work item: `ACT-026`.

## Decision

**A real, live adoption attempt against Plutos — not a probe, and not a scripted fixture — exposed
a data-loss bug and a genuine fit gap between the wizard and the audience it is written for. Both
are addressed. The bug is fixed now, unconditionally. The fit gap is addressed in two phases: Phase
1 (this packet) ships substance fixes on the existing architecture; Phase 2 (scoped, not started)
rebuilds the rendering layer on Textual once Phase 1's content exists to design against.**

1. `F36`: `render.py` hand-built YAML flow sequences (`artefacts: [...]`, `paths: [...]`,
   `enforcement: [...]`, `wired_in: [...]`) by escaping each item as though it were its own
   document, then wrapping brackets around the result by hand — the wrong escaping rules for an
   item inside an existing flow sequence. The maintainer's own answer, `what is this?`, broke this
   exactly, and the wizard's own round-trip check refused to write, with no way to recover roughly
   twenty minutes of already-typed answers. Fixed unconditionally, regardless of anything else this
   record decides, because it is a correctness defect, not a design question.
2. The interaction model `DR-32` chose — sequential `questionary` prompts, reasoned as appropriate
   because "the adopt flow is linear, not a dashboard" — does not match what was actually approved.
   Read back frame-by-frame against the original mockup artifact (`8b82b21c`, "Adopt, Two Ways"),
   the approved design shows a persistent framed interface with already-answered fields staying
   visible, a conformance-level screen that recaps what earlier sections established and lists each
   level's specific controls with an interactive `? why does this matter` affordance, and a gate
   catalogue browsable several items at a time with inline follow-ups and a persistent progress
   indicator. Plain sequential prompts cannot produce this regardless of how the wording changes.
3. Independent of the interaction model, the wizard presumed knowledge its own stated audience does
   not have: the maintainer's own transcript shows three consecutive "don't know what this is"
   answers at the start of the gate catalogue, and 114 unexplained questions (19 gates × up to 6
   fields) produced fatigue and placeholder junk once the maintainer judged the pace unsustainable.
   `core/PREREQUISITE_GATES.md`'s own text — *"the level sets a floor on which gates must be
   `required`"* — means a first-time adopter choosing `standard` without existing governance
   infrastructure is forced into gates they cannot honestly satisfy, the same shape `DR-28` found
   for Plyego, now met head-on by the audience this tool exists for rather than found by a probe.

## Context and alternatives

### What actually happened

The maintainer ran `surfaceplate adopt` against a real, `--no-hooks`-installed Plutos, not a
scripted or probed run. In order: a visual/pacing gap against the agreed mockup; the knowledge-
assumption gap above; fatigue; the data-loss bug (`F36`); and, verbatim: *"Extremely long and
difficult. Potentially giving dropdown or list of options and what they mean would help. I don't
know, really bad experience."* His governing instruction for everything that follows: *"This is all
for the time being. good starting point. Let's discuss first. Until we don't have a complete
remediation plan that I approve you don't implement anything."*

### Why this is a genuine revision of `DR-32`, not merely new work

`DR-32` weighed a full TUI (Textual) against sequential prompts and chose the latter, reasoned
against the shape of the flow rather than against what the approved mockup actually depicted. That
reasoning is now known to be wrong on its own terms: the mockup was never re-read frame-by-frame
against the choice at the time `DR-32` was written, and doing so now shows the chosen architecture
structurally cannot render what was approved, independent of the maintainer's live-session feedback.
`DR-32` is not overturned lightly — the maintainer explicitly weighed the cost (a new dependency, a
rendering rewrite, the largest single packet this project has attempted) against continuing to
patch the existing architecture, and chose to invest, in a structured discussion covering the
interaction model, level-choice steering, mode design, and resumability, each decided in turn:

- **Interaction model** — invest in Textual (Phase 2), reconsidering `DR-32`'s rejection.
- **Conformance-level choice** — stays neutral (repository-dependent, the maintainer's own words:
  *"they are tool/repo dependent"*), but the level screen now shows detected signals (existing CI,
  an existing decisions/docs-shaped folder, an existing CHANGELOG) alongside each level's gate and
  control counts, so the honest cost of a choice is visible before it is made — the same table
  shape `DR-28` and `DR-34` already build for Plyego and Plutos, moved earlier in the flow rather
  than left to be discovered mid-adoption.
- **Mode design** — a `simple`/`advanced` choice, asked once, up front. Both explain; the maintainer
  explicitly rejected treating today's terse, unexplained prompts as an acceptable `advanced`
  register: *"keep in mind that we need much more detail and guidance compared to the one we have
  for advanced as well. don't ever think what we have now would be suitable to advance."* Each of
  the ~35 explainable items (3 baseline controls, up to 9 conformance-level controls across all
  levels, all 19 gates) needs two independently authored explanations — plain English assuming no
  software/governance background for `simple`; precise technical terminology assuming engineering
  literacy but not this framework's own vocabulary for `advanced`.
- **Recognition over recall** — every rationale field gains an editable example default, sourced
  from `examples/application-profile.*.example.yaml`'s existing worked prose, matching the pattern
  `review_by`/`enforcement` already use (a shown default is still an answer, since it must be
  explicitly submitted).
- **Resumability** — `DR-32` deferred this for lack of evidence it was needed. There is now direct
  evidence: a real session lost roughly twenty minutes of answers to a late-stage failure with no
  recovery path. Basic resumability is added: incremental per-section draft saves, offered (never
  silently resumed) on the next invocation, with a version/digest mismatch flagged rather than
  silently trusted.

Three worked examples — `agent_work_packets`, `component_library`, and the level-choice screen
itself, both registers — were drafted and approved before scaling the same voice to the remaining
~32 items. Authoring `agent_work_packets`'s plain-English register surfaced a real finding in its
own right: this framework has no clean one-sentence definition of the term anywhere in its existing
documentation today, a documentation gap the wizard's own content work exposed rather than invented.

### Why two phases, not one

The full scope — dual-register content for ~35 items, a mode choice, detected-signal display,
example-answer defaults, resumability, and a ground-up Textual rebuild of the rendering layer — is
the largest single packet this project has attempted. Splitting it was proposed and accepted at
review, on two grounds: Phase 1 alone addresses what actually caused the live-session pain (jargon,
fatigue, and the data loss) and is deliverable without a new dependency or a rendering rewrite; and
designing Phase 2's Textual screens against content that does not yet exist would be planning
against a guess rather than the real, reviewable material Phase 1 produces.

### Alternatives rejected

- **Patch the wording within the existing sequential-prompt architecture and stop there.** Rejected:
  the mockup comparison shows the architecture itself, not merely its wording, cannot render what
  was approved — a wording patch would leave `DR-32`'s actual error standing on the ceiling the
  architecture itself imposes, not merely on the wording.
- **Rebuild on Textual immediately, in one packet.** Rejected: it would mean designing screens
  against ~35 items of content that does not exist yet, and would delay the correctness fix (`F36`)
  and the substance fixes (mode, detected signals, examples, resumability) behind a much larger,
  higher-risk rewrite that doesn't need to gate them.
- **Have the wizard pick a conformance level from detected signals.** Rejected by the maintainer
  directly: level choice stays a genuinely neutral, repository-dependent human decision; the tool's
  job is to make the cost of each option visible, not to choose for the human.

## Impact

- **Numerical/model output:** none.
- **Contracts/schemas:** none changed by this record. `application-profile.schema.yaml` is
  unaffected; the fix and the remediation both operate on how the wizard asks and renders, not on
  what a valid profile contains.
- **Security/data:** none.
- **Operations/release:** `F36`'s fix changes what every future adopter's written profile looks
  like whenever a free-text answer contains a YAML-flow-sequence-special character — previously a
  silent write refusal with no recovery, now a correct write. The rest of Phase 1 changes what
  every future adopter is asked and how — a mode choice, dual-register explanations, detected
  signals, example answers, and resumability; Phase 2, once built, changes how it is rendered.
- **`org/RELEASE_PLAN.md` item 2** is amended by this record: item 2 shipped the CLI and wizard;
  this record is the first defect and design revision found from a real, unscripted use of it.

## Limitations and follow-up

- **Phase 2 (the Textual rendering rewrite) is scoped, not planned in implementation detail.** It
  requires its own Explore/Design pass once Phase 1's content is real material to design against,
  and is explicitly not part of this record's Impact section.
- **The re-run above is scripted evidence, not a live session.** It proves the mechanism works —
  explanations shown, signals detected, the bug fixed, the level choice left neutral — not that a
  non-technical adopter would find the result genuinely easy going. Only a real session, like the
  one that raised this record, can establish that; none has happened since this fix.
- **Recognition over recall is proven at the unit and coverage level, not visually in the re-run
  transcript above.** `ScriptedPrompt` does not display a `default=` value the way a real terminal
  would, so the example answers' actual on-screen appearance is unverified by the transcript,
  though their content and coverage are.
