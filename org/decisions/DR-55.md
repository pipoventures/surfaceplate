## Decision

1. Every gate and control whose artefact can be a true, complete file on the day it is written
   has a seed and the "create it" row: seven more gates (the options log, the risk
   classification, the test conventions, the data-source register, the output-validation log,
   the dependency-review log, the release checklist) and the four record-directory controls
   (`method_registry`, `overrides`, `run_lineage`, `provenance`), for which the reference is the
   directory and the seed is a note inside it, which the checker does not read.
2. A seed is an empty register or log that says it is empty, or a statement of the standard's
   own rule that says what this repository has not yet declared. It is never a template: no
   placeholder tokens, nothing to fill in to make it true. The three "stated" seeds quote the
   standard (its four-point risk scale and its audit triggers; its recommended test naming; the
   records its gates require before release) and leave the repository's own part visibly blank.
3. What stays without a seed, on purpose: the four interface gates, `regression_before_merge`,
   `equivalence_evidence`, `authority_same_change` and `register_currency` (they reuse the map
   and the register), the lock file, and the two CI-step controls. Each of those can only be the
   adopter's own.
4. Two controls that share a directory (`provenance`, `run_lineage`) share one seed; choosing
   both creates one note.

## Context and alternatives

The maintainer asked whether the row applies everywhere it might be needed. The alternative,
a seed for every artefact, was set aside because a seed that has to be filled in is a template,
and `DR-43` records why a template fails the checker on the next run and satisfies a gate with a
file nobody wrote.

## Impact

- Numerical/model output: none. Contracts/schemas: none.
- Reproducibility/lineage: none; the record-directory seeds hold no records.
- Operations/release: eleven new files in `seeds/`, which is not vendored; the audit bundle
  carries them.

## Evidence

- `ACT-053`, test-first: every seed writes, holds no placeholder, and says it holds nothing; a
  `full` run choosing every row writes them all and the checker passes in full.

## Limitations and follow-up

The risk classification and release checklist seeds are only as true as their blanks: a
repository that adopts them and never declares its level meanings is classifying against an
undeclared scheme, and the seed says so. No check enforces that the blanks are ever filled.
