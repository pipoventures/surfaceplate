## Decision proposed

**Replace the `core/` ÷ `agent-instructions/` split with a single twelve-topic axis**, so that
class, audience and enforcement become **fields on a rule** rather than properties of the file it
sits in.

| # | Topic | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Authority and the documentary record | Where does truth live, and what happens when two documents disagree? |
| 2 | Decision authority and escalation | What may I decide, and what must I stop for? |
| 3 | Work definition | What is this task, and where does it end? |
| 4 | Work tracking | What is owed here, and what state is it in? |
| 5 | Risk and proportionality | How much control does *this* change need? |
| 6 | Evidence and completion | What counts as done, and how do I report it honestly? |
| 7 | Testing | What do tests prove, and which do I run? |
| 8 | Confidentiality and data boundaries | What must never leave, and what must never enter? |
| 9 | Dependencies and supply chain | What comes in from outside, and on what terms? |
| 10 | Provenance and lineage | Where did this output come from? |
| 11 | Concurrency and shared-repository discipline | How do several agents share one repository? |
| 12 | Enforcement and change control | How are these rules checked, and how do they change? |

Each topic is **one authored document carrying a normative part and an imperative part**, and the
existing emitter is extended to ship **only the imperative part** into agent destinations.

## The evidence for the change, measured rather than asserted

The corpus is **13 documents, 1,423 lines**. The duplication the change exists to remove is
visible without interpretation: `core/AI_OPERATING_MODEL.md` and
`standard/agent-instructions/ai-workflow.md` carry **three headings that are word-for-word
identical** — *Evidence-first completion*, *Audit triggers*, *Proportionality* — and two more that
are the same subject under different names (*Work contract* / *Every task needs a work packet*;
*Human-only decisions* / *Roles* + *Stop and ask*). Five of `AI_OPERATING_MODEL.md`'s six sections
have a twin. That is what an audience-layer axis produces, and it is not an accident to be tidied.

**Topics are stable; class, audience and enforcement are not.** A default becomes a gate, a rule
that bound only agents comes to bind humans, a convention becomes machine-checked. Wherever the
filesystem encodes a changing attribute, every such change is a file move. Under a topic axis they
are field edits in place.

## Three problems this record must settle before any file moves

### 1. The corpus is not evenly distributed, and the brief did not measure it

`core/CONFORMANCE_LEVELS.md` (299 lines) and `core/PREREQUISITE_GATES.md` (322 lines) are
**621 lines — 43% of the whole corpus** — and both are topic 12. Under the brief's scheme a level
becomes "a floor within each topic", which distributes `CONFORMANCE_LEVELS.md` across all twelve,
and the 19-gate catalogue distributes with it.

**Proposed:** the twelve topic documents are **rule** documents. `PREREQUISITE_GATES.md` and
`CONFORMANCE_LEVELS.md` remain as **specifications** — a different kind of artefact, cited by the
topic documents and citing them back, carrying the catalogue, the level table and the generated
finding-code index. What moves into the topic documents is each topic's **floor statement**; what
stays is the machine-readable specification the checker implements.

The alternative — dissolving both documents into twelve — is rejected because it would scatter a
single coherent specification across twelve files and make the checker's own data structures
answerable to twelve documents instead of two. That is more file moves, not fewer, and it is the
opposite of the change's rationale.

### 2. "Twelve" is already taken, inside a machine-checked sentence

`core/CONFORMANCE_LEVELS.md:67` reads **"Ten of the twelve controls this framework defines are
checked"**, and `tests/check_code_registers.py:198` asserts that exact sentence against
`len(CONFORMANCE_LEVELS["full"]) + 3` and `len(VERIFIED_CONTROLS | {"secret_hygiene"})`,
number-words included. `:156` says "the twelve" again. After this change the corpus contains
**twelve topics and twelve controls**, and they are unrelated sets that happen to be the same size.

**Proposed:** the bare word "twelve" is never used alone in the shipped corpus. Every occurrence is
"the twelve topics" or "the twelve controls", and `check_code_registers.py` gains an assertion that
no shipped document contains "twelve" unqualified. The collision is a naming hazard, not a reason
to change either number.

### 3. There is no `SP` code registry, and the fifth surface needs one first

The 56 codes are **string literals** passed to `Finding(...)` and recovered by regex
(`check_code_registers.py:40`). "Finding codes gain topic membership as a field" therefore requires
building a registry that does not exist. This is the hidden cost in the fifth surface and the
reason it is sequenced last below.

**Proposed:** a registry mapping each code to its topic, with `check_code_registers.py`'s five
existing `SP` assertions passing unchanged, and a sixth added: every emitted code has a topic, and
every topic named is one of the twelve. **No code is renumbered** — `SP001-SP035`,
`SP037-SP043`, `SP046-SP059`, gap `SP036`, reserved `SP044-SP045`.

## Boundaries settled

- **3 versus 4** — settled: the **work packet** is topic 3 (work definition); the **register, its
  identifiers and its statuses** are topic 4 (work tracking).
- **5 versus 6** — the brief left this contested. **Proposed:** risk classification,
  proportionality and the codification threshold are topic 5; evidence labels, the review packet,
  completion reporting and failure discipline are topic 6. The test that separates them: topic 5
  decides *how much control this change needs before it starts*; topic 6 decides *what may be
  claimed about it afterwards*.
- **The seven workflow skills stay on their own axis** and are not folded in. `bug-fix`, `change`,
  `dependency-update`, `fix-ci`, `release`, `review`, `security-review` are organised by *task*;
  topics by *subject*. A workflow cites topics. Collapsing them recreates the same many-to-many
  problem one level up.

## Three constraints that must survive, quoted so they can be checked afterwards

1. `core/CONFORMANCE_LEVELS.md:30` — *"a repository can declare both, do neither, and pass."*
2. `README.md:221` — *"weaker than prevention and stronger than most things called enforcement."*
3. **No adopter-facing claim may get stronger because the documents got tidier.**

A restructure that quietly strengthened a claim while moving it would be the single worst outcome
available here, and these three are the specific wordings to diff against afterwards.

## The five surfaces, and the order proposed

| | Surface | Carries | Breaking? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Documents** — twelve topic files; emitter extended to ship the imperative part only | `F120` (level-graded rules), `F125` (precedence), `WI-1` (`DR-2`'s scope) | No, pre-1.0 |
| 2 | **Application profile** — per-topic decisions replace the flat control list | `WI-2` (adopter-declared canon), `F121` (`owner_role` at `essential`), and `H20`'s opt-out if taken | **Yes** — `schema_version` moves off `"1.0"` here and only here |
| 3 | **Wizard** — question order becomes topic order, floor before options | `DR-58`'s coverage requirement unchanged | No |
| 4 | **Conformance levels** — a level becomes a floor within each topic | | No |
| 5 | **Finding codes** — the registry, then topic membership as a field | | No |

Surface 2 is the only breaking change, which is why `WI-2` was ruled to ride with it: one break to
a published contract rather than two.

## Impact

- **Contracts/schemas:** breaking, once, at surface 2. Every adopter re-pins. There is one adopting
  repository today and it is the maintainer's own, which is the whole argument for doing this now.
- **Security/data:** none directly.
- **Reproducibility:** the emitter gains section selection. Its interaction with `DR-45`'s
  manifest/digest verification is **not yet examined** — see Limitations.
- **Operations:** the largest single change this project has made.

## Limitations and follow-up

**The emitter's section selection is untested against `DR-45`.** The manifest and the install
record cover payload files; an emitter that ships *part* of a document changes what is hashed on
the way in, and that interaction has not been examined. It is the first thing surface 1 must
establish, and if it does not hold, the document shape decided in the 2026-09-05 ruling has to be
revisited rather than worked around.

**The review packet must not be sent against a half-restructured corpus.** The operator's ruling
gates this work on that, and the packet currently distributed is for the published `0.16.1`, which
is pre-restructure and remains valid for that release.

**This record settles an axis, not twelve documents' contents.** Each topic document is a separate
piece of authoring, and any of them may surface a boundary this record got wrong. That is expected;
what is not acceptable is silently re-drawing a boundary rather than amending this record.
