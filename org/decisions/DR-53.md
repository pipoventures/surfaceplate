## Decision

1. The opening screen carries a mark: a surface plate in isometric, facing left, with the
   monogram `SP` on its top face. The slab is drawn with four box-drawing characters and the
   letters with the full block only, the one glyph every monospace font renders identically.
2. The mark is generated from its geometry (`tui/mark.py`: the top face's inner width and the
   slab's thickness in rows), never drawn by hand: the right face is exactly the thickness deep
   and its bottom slant runs parallel to the top edge for the full length. Both were wrong in
   the hand-drawn preview and the maintainer saw it.
3. The tool line and the tagline sit beside the slab; the slab's edges are the muted ink and the
   letters the accent. The screen's frame has no vertical padding, so the mark, the facts and
   the closing paragraph fit 24 rows at 80 columns with one spare row for a note about a draft.
4. The opening screen's golden snapshot holds the mark; a change to it carries cause, delta and
   reviewer like every golden.

## Context and alternatives

`F89`: *"Home page is not visual enough. Something resembling a logo or something more visual
(like a geometry) would be nice there."* Four alternatives were offered: the slab, a datum line
with ticks, a block-letter monogram, a framed level. The maintainer asked for the monogram inside
the slab, and for a preview before implementation. Half-block letterforms were rejected because
they render differently across fonts.

## Limitations and follow-up

The slanted edge characters are East Asian ambiguous-width code points; in a terminal or font
that renders them double width the slab's edges will not align. The maintainer's terminal
rendered them correctly; no other has been tried.
