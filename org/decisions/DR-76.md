## Decision

**`DR-69` said a conformance level would become "a floor within each topic rather than a flat
control set", with `CONFORMANCE_LEVELS` and `LEVEL_REQUIRED_GATES` re-keyed by topic. They are
not, and this record amends that surface.**

1. **The checker's structures stay flat.** `CONFORMANCE_LEVELS` remains `level → {control}`.
2. **The levels document gains a generated view**: *What each level requires, by topic*, built
   from `CONFORMANCE_LEVELS` and `rules.CONTROL_TOPICS` by
   `tests/check_code_registers.py --write`, and failed in CI when it drifts.
3. **The three baseline controls get their own column.** They are required at every level and so
   appear in no level's set; omitting them made two topics read as rows of em-dashes.
4. **A bare "twelve" is a check.** In the shipped corpus "twelve" names three different things —
   twelve controls, twelve topics, twelve cross-application control principles.

## Context and alternatives

**Rejected: re-keying `CONFORMANCE_LEVELS` by topic, as `DR-69` specified.** Read before deciding:
the structure has **58 usages across 16 files**, and every one asks *"is this control required at
this level"*. Not one asks which topics a level touches. Re-keying would have changed all of them
to obtain an answer nothing needed.

Worse, it would hold the topic mapping **twice inside the framework** — once in
`rules.CONTROL_TOPICS`, once in the level structure — with nothing comparing them. That is `F130`'s
shape exactly, raised four commits ago: one fact, several declarations, and a green build while
they disagree. Introducing it deliberately, in the same session that closed it, would have been the
clearer error.

**This is `DR-71`'s reasoning transposed, and the transposition needs stating rather than assuming.**
`DR-71` rejected nesting controls under topics in the *adopter's profile* because a control's topic
is the framework's fact and the adopter would restate it. Here the file is the framework's own, so
that argument does not apply and a different one does: not *"the adopter would restate our fact"*
but *"we would restate our own"*. Both land on the same place, which `DR-71` put well: **the goal
was never nesting — it was that a reader sees twelve named subjects.** A generated view delivers
that completely, and storage was never the part the reader experiences.

**Rejected: doing nothing.** Before this, no document answered *"what does Topic 7 require of me at
`standard`?"* A reader had to hold the level's control list against the topic map in their head.
The view is new information, not decoration.

**Gates keep their six-group catalogue order**, per `DR-71`'s scope note. They group nineteen gates
on a different axis from the twelve topics, and re-cutting them was declined two surfaces ago.
The generated view says so, so a reader does not read an em-dash as "nothing is required here".

## Impact

- `surfaceplate/core/CONFORMANCE_LEVELS.md` gains one generated block. It is payload, so every
  adopter's installed copy gains it too.
- `tests/check_code_registers.py` gains `render_levels_by_topic`, its generate-and-check pair, and
  the bare-`twelve` assertion. 105 checks → 122.
- No code path changes. `VERIFIED_CONTROLS` and the A/B/C/D verification patterns are untouched,
  as `DR-69` required.

## Evidence

- The view regenerates and matches; editing either the checker's levels or the topic map without
  regenerating fails the suite, which is the property that makes a generated document worth having.
- The bare-`twelve` check **found one on its first run**: *"Every control that leaves an inspectable
  artefact is checked — ten of the twelve, as stated above"*, in the very document that now also
  carries a twelve-**topic** table. Corrected to "ten of the twelve controls", and the check
  verified in both directions — reinstating the bare form fails it.
- `check_code_registers.py`'s existing checked-controls sentence assertion still resolves, which
  `DR-69` named as this surface's regression risk.

## Limitations and follow-up

**The view covers selectable controls and not gates.** A topic with an em-dash in every column may
still be obliged by prerequisite gates, and the generated text says so rather than leaving a reader
to infer that an empty row means an empty obligation. Mapping the nineteen gates to topics would
complete the picture and is **not** done here: it is new framework knowledge, not a rearrangement
of existing knowledge, and `DR-71` declined re-cutting the gate axis.

**The bare-`twelve` check is a guard, not a fix.** Every other use in the corpus was already
qualified. It holds a property that was true and easy to lose, which is the cheapest kind of check
to add and the easiest kind of defect to reintroduce.
