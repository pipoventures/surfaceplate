# Decision Record

- Decision ID: DR-46
- Date: 2026-09-02
- Application: Surfaceplate (the framework itself) — the wizard's binding rule, a normative document,
  and what an adopting repository's profile discloses about its own controls.
- Decision owner: Mario Pipo (maintainer), per the decision-maker convention recorded in
  `org/decisions/README.md`. **The `effective_from` remedy was chosen by him from three options**,
  because it changes the project's most-cited rule.
- Status: accepted — implemented in the same change (`ACT-038`).
- Risk level: 4 — it amends the binding rule every packet since `ACT-020` has been measured against,
  corrects a published normative document, and changes what every adopting repository's profile says.
- Related work item: `ACT-038`. Closes `F51`, `F52`, `F53`, `F54`. Raises `F55`.

## Decision

**Act on the cross-provider review's findings on their merits, one at a time. Accept none because a
reviewer asserted it; dismiss none because its evidence was imperfect.**

1. **`effective_from` is asked again**, and the binding rule is amended to state exactly what the
   tool may supply and what it never may.
2. **`CONFORMANCE_LEVELS.md`'s absolute claim is corrected**, and the superseded sentence kept in
   place, marked.
3. **Every control in a rendered profile says whether the framework checks it**, derived from
   `VERIFIED_CONTROLS` — which was itself corrected first.
4. **The review packet's own two defects are fixed.**
5. **The verdict is not adopted as authority.** `FAIL` is the reviewer's conclusion on 11 files;
   what follows from it is the maintainer's.

## Context and alternatives

### What the review was, and what it was worth

`RELEASE_PLAN` item 9 exists on the argument that *"two instruments asking different questions… is
how nearly every finding in `org/FINDINGS.md` was actually found."* It returned `FAIL` and produced
**three findings that hold, one right about the risk and wrong about the evidence, and one
judgement**. Every checkable claim was verified against the code before being accepted or rejected.

The two most valuable were ones no internal instrument had a reason to look for:

- **`F51`** — a contradiction between a *published rule* and an *implementation*. The tests could not
  see it because they were written against the implementation. Finding it required reading two
  documents against each other with no stake in either.
- **`F53`** — a question asked from the adopter's chair rather than the maintainer's: *can the person
  reading this profile tell which of these controls is real?* Everything internal had been asking
  whether the profile was correct, which it was.

`F53` then surfaced a defect the reviewer could not have seen: `VERIFIED_CONTROLS` omitted
`secret_hygiene`, which `SP046`/`SP047` verify. **The checker under-reported itself**, and the fix for
the reviewer's finding would have propagated that error into every adopter's profile had it been
built on the set uncorrected.

### Why the rule was amended and not simply obeyed

Three options were put to the maintainer. He chose to **ask `effective_from` again *and* tighten the
rule**, and the reasoning matters more than the choice.

*"It never sets a date"* was **already false when it was written**. `adoption_date` has been a
tool-supplied date since the first version of this wizard — disclosed in `build_adoption`'s docstring
and named in `tests/test_provenance.py`'s allow-list, but not in the rule. So the rule read as
absolute over a documented carve-out, and `ACT-032` widened the gap by deriving a second date.
Reverting alone would have restored compliance with a sentence that was still inaccurate, leaving the
next person to trip on it.

The rule now names three things the tool may write unasked — a fact of record, a value from the
install record, this framework's own published prose — and three it never may: a conformance level, a
rationale, a scope decision. **`tests/test_provenance.py` is named in the rule as the enforcement**,
so a change needing the allow-list to grow is a change to the rule, and belongs in a decision record
before it belongs in code.

### The argument the reviewer did not make, and it is the stronger one

`SP033` refuses a future `effective_from`; `SP034` refuses moving one forward. A human's answer can
therefore only ever *widen or equal* the audit window. **Deriving "now" silently selected the
narrowest value the rules permit**, on the field that decides how much history the gate audit
examines. The fallback also made the substitution unobservable — a missing answer and an answer of
today produced an identical profile.

That is a stronger reason to ask than rule compliance, and it survives even if someone disagrees that
the rule was breached.

### What was rejected, and why that is not defensiveness

**"`scaffold.py` writes empty files."** The seeds are 852–2,396 bytes of prose, each opening by
stating that its own existence is not the practice. The claim is false — **and the packet caused it**:
the bundle asked whether creating an artefact makes a gate pass and did not attach the artefacts.
Recorded as `F54`, a defect in the hand-off, not a reviewer error. The concern underneath it is
legitimate and unchanged: `DR-43` records it as the risk the whole capability carries.

**"Strip the historical essays from the docstrings."** Not done, and `F55` records why on both sides.
The drift is real — two docstrings were found wrong about their own code the previous day, both
corrected. The counter is that those comments have repeatedly carried the reasoning that prevented a
repeat, and this project has already measured what happens when guidance sits where nobody reads it:
`F29`, 501 lines of agent instruction loaded by nothing. The remedy is a habit, not a deletion, and
it stays open because it is not mechanically enforceable.

### The most consequential sentence in the report was about the reviewer

> *"I am unable to mathematically compute the raw SHA-256 byte digest of the provided text file."*

Honest, correct, and it means **item 9 did not narrow `F6` at all**. The recomputation was the entire
reason `MANIFEST.sha256` was attached, and it is the step `F6` names as its closing condition. The
packet was asking a text-only model for a hash — which invites a fabricated one from anyone less
careful, in an integrity review. Now scoped to a reviewer with code execution, with everyone else
told to report a gap and explicitly told not to estimate.

**`F6` needs a party who can compute, not only read.** That is a person with a shell, or a reviewer
with tools — a distinction this repository had not previously drawn.

### Alternatives rejected

- **Adopt the `FAIL` verdict as a gate.** A reviewer's verdict is evidence. Treating it as authority
  would make an external party the release decision, which `org/decisions/README.md` reserves.
- **Revert all four `ACT-032` derivations** for literal compliance. Measured at roughly +44 questions
  at `full` with a UI, and it would return the adopter to paraphrasing prose the framework ships.
  Only `effective_from` is a scope decision; the other three are consequences.
- **Amend the rule without asking `effective_from` again.** Cheapest, and it would have changed the
  most-cited rule in the project immediately after an external reviewer found the gap, which reads as
  moving the goalposts — and would have kept the tool choosing the narrowest audit window.

## Impact

- **Numerical/model output:** none.
- **Contracts/schemas:** none. `effective_from` is asked rather than derived; the profile gains
  comments, not fields.
- **Security/dependencies:** none.
- **Operations/release:** every adopter is asked **+1 / +4 / +15** questions at `essential` /
  `standard` / `full`+UI, and every rendered profile now states, per control, whether the framework
  checks it.

## Evidence

- Eleven suites pass: contracts 173, installer 190, adopt 88, provenance 6, adopt-tui 44, render 19,
  discover 18, scaffold 21, identifiers 191, registers 10 (55 F codes), vendored 57 + 1 delegated.
- **`F51` seen to fail:** the assertions reported *"fields asked: ['artefact', 'paths']"* and
  *"the tool supplied '2026-09-02' on the human's behalf"*.
- **`F53` seen to fail:** with `VERIFIED_CONTROLS` reverted, `secret_hygiene` renders as
  *"DECLARED ONLY - nothing checks this"* — a checked control labelled trusted, which is the whole
  defect the label exists to prevent.
- **The provenance allow-list did not grow.** The assurance note is a YAML comment; the walk parses
  YAML and never sees it. `PROVENANCE=PASS (6 checks)`, unchanged.
- **The bundle command was run:** 15 files, 2,685 lines, 141 KB, with all four seeds present.
- The review as delivered is kept verbatim at `audit/REVIEW_2026-09-01_CROSS_PROVIDER.md`, including
  the parts that were wrong.

## Limitations and follow-up

- **`F6` is not advanced.** Item 9 ran and did not perform the one step that would narrow it.
- **This was one reviewer, one pass, 11 files.** The checker and installer — the largest omission —
  were not reviewed, and `RELEASE_PLAN` schedules a second pass after real adopters for that reason.
- **`F55` stays open** with no mechanical remedy.
- **The corrected packet has not been run.** Its two defects were only visible once used, and there
  is no reason to think a third defect is not waiting on the same terms.
- **Nobody has yet adopted with `effective_from` asked again**, so the +15 at `full` is a measured
  count and not an observed experience.
