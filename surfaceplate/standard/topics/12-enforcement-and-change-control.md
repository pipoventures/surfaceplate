# Topic 12 — Enforcement and change control

Installed by Surfaceplate. Do not edit this file in an adopting repository.

*How are these rules checked, and how do they change?*

This topic is a **floor statement**, deliberately. Its detail lives in two specifications this
document cites rather than reproduces: `core/CONFORMANCE_LEVELS.md` (what each conformance level
requires, and which of its controls are actually verified rather than merely declared) and
`core/PREREQUISITE_GATES.md` (the catalogue of gates — rules of the shape *"X must exist before Y
may begin"* — and how each is checked). Between them they are 43% of this standard's authored
content by line count; folding them into twelve topic files would scatter one coherent
specification across twelve documents and make the checker's own data structures answerable to
twelve files instead of two. They stay whole.

## What every topic's floor means

Every other topic in this restructure states a **floor** — the minimum that binds regardless of
conformance level — with more expected as the level rises. That grading is what
`core/CONFORMANCE_LEVELS.md` makes precise for the controls it defines, and what Topic 4 (Work
tracking) demonstrates concretely for a register. **A repository can declare a control both
required and unverified, and pass** — declaring is not the same as the checker having a way to
confirm it, and `core/CONFORMANCE_LEVELS.md`'s own text says so plainly rather than leaving a
reader to assume otherwise.

## A schema file is not enforcement

Enforcement exists only where validation is invoked and its tests actually run. A schema that
*could* reject a bad profile but is never run against one enforces nothing. The same is true of a
gate: a gate can be blocked locally and made impossible to hide afterwards, which is weaker than
prevention and stronger than most things called enforcement — the honest ceiling this standard
states about itself rather than overclaims.

## How change happens

A control's presence, a gate's catalogue entry, and a finding code's meaning are each a published
contract. Changing what one asserts is a decision recorded per Topic 1 (Authority): a durable,
append-only record identifying the decision, the alternatives, the rationale, the accountable
human, the date, and the affected authorities. Raising a conformance level is a normal versioned
change; lowering one is a material control decision, because it reduces assurance over outputs
that may already be relied upon, and the rationale for doing so must be recorded.

## For agents — Enforcement and change control
